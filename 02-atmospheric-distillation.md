# Stage 2: Atmospheric distillation (CDU)

The crude distillation unit splits the crude into fractions by boiling range. It is a separation step: no chemical reaction happens here.

![Atmospheric column with Oman crude cut yields](../assets/fig02_atmospheric_column.png)

## How it works

- Desalted crude is heated in the **preheat train**, then in a **fired furnace** to about **350-370 °C**, where it partly vaporises.
- It enters the **flash zone** near the bottom of a tall column fitted with trays (often sieve or valve trays) or packing.
- **Vapour rises and liquid falls.** Each tray is a stage where vapour and liquid approach equilibrium, so lighter components concentrate higher up.
- **Side draws** take products out at set heights, and small **side strippers** use steam to remove light ends from each cut.
- **Stripping steam** at the bottom lowers the partial pressure and strips light components from the residue.
- **Pumparounds** remove heat from the middle of the column. This recovers energy into the preheat train and improves separation.
- At the top, vapour is **condensed**. Part returns as **reflux** and the rest leaves as naphtha and gas.

## Enters with / leaves with

| Stream | Boiling range | Oman yield, wt% | Leaves to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In:** desalted crude | whole crude | 100 | — |
| Gas + LPG | <15 °C | 1.1 | gas plant, amine, LPG |
| Light naphtha | 15-80 °C | 4.3 | isomerisation, gasoline blending |
| Heavy naphtha | 80-150 °C | 8.7 | naphtha hydrotreater, then reformer |
| Kerosene | 150-230 °C | 10.1 | kerosene hydrotreater or Merox, then jet A-1 |
| Gas oil / diesel | 230-375 °C | 21.1 | diesel hydrotreater |
| Atmospheric residue | >375 °C | 54.7 | vacuum distillation |

Yields: TotalEnergies Oman crude assay. Real cut points vary by refinery design and by which product the refinery wants to maximise.

## Key operating variables

| Variable | Effect |
|---|---|
| Furnace outlet temperature | higher = more distillate, but risk of cracking above about 370 °C |
| Reflux ratio | higher = sharper separation, more energy |
| Stripping steam rate | more steam = lighter residue, more sour water |
| Column pressure | slightly above atmospheric; lower pressure helps vaporisation |

*Ranges are typical industry values.*
