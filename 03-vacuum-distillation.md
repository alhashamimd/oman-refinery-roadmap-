# Stage 3: Vacuum distillation (VDU)

Above about 350-370 °C, hydrocarbons begin to crack thermally, forming coke and gums. The atmospheric residue still holds a lot of valuable gas oil, so it is distilled **under vacuum**, where boiling points are lower and heavier cuts can be recovered without thermal damage.

## How it works

- Atmospheric residue is reheated in a **vacuum furnace** and fed to a large-diameter column.
- The column runs at about **20-50 mmHg absolute** (typical), created by **steam ejectors** or liquid-ring pumps.
- **Packing or low-pressure-drop trays** keep the pressure low through the column.
- **Vacuum gas oil (VGO)** is drawn off the side. **Vacuum residue** leaves from the bottom.

## Enters with / leaves with (Oman crude)

| Stream | Boiling range | Yield, wt% of crude | Sulfur, wt% | Density, kg/m³ | Goes to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **In:** atmospheric residue | >375 °C | 54.7 | 2.29 | 954 | — |
| Vacuum gas oil | 375-565 °C | 26.4 | 1.49 | 914 | FCC or hydrocracker |
| Vacuum residue | >565 °C | 28.3 | 3.04 | 995 | delayed coker, visbreaker, bitumen, fuel oil |

Source: TotalEnergies Oman crude assay.

## Why it matters for Oman

More than half of an Oman crude barrel boils above 375 °C. Without the VDU and conversion units, that half could only be sold as heavy fuel oil. This is why Sohar added a delayed coker and hydrocracker, and why Duqm was designed around a hydrocracker and a coker.

## Key limits on the products

| Product | What is controlled | Why |
|---|---|---|
| VGO | metals (Ni + V), carbon residue, end point | protects FCC and hydrocracker catalysts |
| Vacuum residue | cut point | sets how much VGO is recovered and how heavy the coker feed is |

*Pressures are typical industry ranges.*
