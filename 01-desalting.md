# Stage 1: Desalting and dehydration

Even after upstream pretreatment, crude arrives with water and dissolved salts. If they stay in the crude, they cause corrosion (chlorides hydrolyse to HCl in the overhead) and fouling in the preheat train and furnace. So desalting is the first processing step.

## How it works

1. **Wash water** is mixed into the crude, usually at a few percent of crude volume, to dissolve the salt.
2. **Demulsifier** is injected to weaken the film that keeps water droplets suspended.
3. A **mixing valve** creates a controlled emulsion so the wash water contacts the brine.
4. In the **electrostatic desalter**, an electric field makes small droplets merge (coalesce) into larger ones that settle by gravity.
5. Salty water (brine) leaves from the bottom to effluent treatment. Desalted crude leaves from the top.

Two stages in series are common, with fresh water entering the second stage and its water recycled to the first.

## Enters with / leaves with

| | Oman crude example | Typical target |
|---|---|---|
| **Salt** | 59 mg/kg (≈ 18 ptb) | ≈ 1 ptb (≈ 3 mg/kg) or lower |
| **Water** | 0.1 vol% (plus wash water added) | ≤ 0.1-0.3 vol% leaving the desalter |
| **Temperature** | from preheat | usually about 120-150 °C, for low viscosity |
| **Brine (out)** | — | low oil-in-water, sent to effluent treatment |

## Chemicals used

| Chemical | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Demulsifier | breaks the oil-water emulsion |
| Wash water | dissolves salt |
| Caustic (sometimes, downstream) | neutralises remaining chlorides |
| Neutralising amine / ammonia (CDU overhead) | controls HCl corrosion if chlorides get through |

## Field note

At OXY (upstream), much of this work is done during stabilisation and pretreatment, before the crude reaches the refinery. The refinery desalter is the last line of defence.

*Ranges on this page are typical industry values, not the design of a specific Omani unit.*
